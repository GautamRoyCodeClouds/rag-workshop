Level 1 · 15 minutes
01

Why RAG
exists at all.

Before we touch a vector, we need to be precise about the problem. What exactly does a language model not know, and why is retraining it the wrong reflex?

The problem

Three things your model genuinely cannot know

01 · TIME

Anything recent

Training stopped on a date. Yesterday's incident report, this morning's price change, last week's policy update: all invisible.

02 · ACCESS

Anything private

Your wiki, your tickets, your contracts, your codebase. None of it was in the training data, and you would not want it to be.

03 · SELF-DOUBT

That it does not know

A model produces fluent text whether or not it has the facts. Confidence is not correlated with correctness.

The reframe

A language model is not a database. It is a reasoning engine with a frozen, lossy memory. RAG stops asking it to remember and starts giving it something to read.

Watch it fail

The failure looks exactly like a success

WITHOUT RETRIEVAL

USER: How many annual leave days do I get?

You are entitled to 20 days of paid annual leave per year, with the option to carry over up to 10 unused days.

⚠ Fluent. Specific. Entirely invented.

WITH RETRIEVAL

USER: How many annual leave days do I get?

You get 24 days of paid annual leave, accruing monthly. Up to 5 unused days carry into the next year.

✓ Grounded in HR-Policy.pdf §4.1, and citable.

The second answer is not from a smarter model. It is the same model, handed the right paragraph.

The fork in the road

Two ways to give a model knowledge

Fine-tuningRAG
What it changesThe model weightsThe prompt, at query time
Good atStyle, format, tone, task shapeFacts, recency, private documents
Update costRetrain and redeployWrite one row to a database
FreshnessStale the day it shipsAs fresh as your last sync
CitationsImpossibleBuilt in, you know the source
Access controlBaked in for everyoneFilter per user at query time
Rule of thumb

Fine-tuning teaches the model how to speak. Retrieval tells it what is true right now. If your complaint is "it does not know our stuff", you want retrieval.

The definition

Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Find the few passages most likely to answer the question, paste them into the prompt, and ask the model to answer using only those.

CLOSED BOOK

Plain LLM

Recites from memory. Fills gaps with plausible invention. No way to check the work.

OPEN BOOK

RAG

Still reasons and writes, but reads the facts off the page. You can check every claim.

Where the engineering lives

The model was never the hard part. Choosing which page to open is the hard part. Levels 2 to 5 are entirely about that choice.

Two clocks

Indexing is a batch job. Querying is a hot path.

OFFLINE · INDEXING

Runs on a schedule

  • Minutes to hours is fine
  • Costs money once per document
  • Triggered by cron, webhook or a manual resync
  • Failure means stale answers, not a broken page
ONLINE · QUERYING

Runs while they wait

  • Budget is a few hundred milliseconds
  • Costs money once per question
  • Triggered by a user pressing Enter
  • Failure means a visibly broken product
Gotcha

The two paths must use the same embedding model. Index with one model and query with another and your similarity scores become noise. Pin the model name and dimension in config, never as a default argument.

Level 2 · 20 minutes
02

Embeddings:
meaning as coordinates.

By the end of this level, "semantic search" stops being a marketing phrase and becomes arithmetic you could do on paper.

The representation problem

Computers compare numbers, not meaning

NAIVE · ONE-HOT 
dog      = [1,0,0,0]
puppy    = [0,1,0,0]
mortgage = [0,0,1,0]

Every word is equally far from every other word. dog is no closer to puppy than to mortgage. Meaning is nowhere in this.

LEARNED · EMBEDDING 
dog      = [0.82, 0.40]
puppy    = [0.86, 0.33]
mortgage = [-0.92,-0.22]

Trained so that text appearing in similar contexts lands in similar positions. Now closeness means relatedness, and closeness is measurable.

An embedding model is a function: text in, a fixed-length list of floats out. Same text always gives the same vector.

The payoff

Related text clusters. Nobody labelled the clusters.

  • Pets upper right, vehicles upper left, finance lower left, water below.
  • No human wrote these categories. They emerged from how the words are used.
  • This is why a search for vacation can find a paragraph that only says annual leave.
Honest caveat

Real embeddings have 768 to 3072 dimensions, not 2. This picture is a flattened shadow of a space you cannot draw. The property it shows is real.

The arithmetic

Cosine similarity: the angle, not the distance

def cosine(a, b):
    dot  = sum(x*y for x,y in zip(a,b))
    return dot / (norm(a) * norm(b))

# if vectors are already unit length:
def cosine_fast(a, b):
    return sum(x*y for x,y in zip(a,b))
Why cosine and not distance

Cosine ignores vector length, so a two-line note and a two-page memo about the same topic still score as similar. Euclidean distance would penalise the longer one.

ScoreReading
0.95 to 1.00Near duplicate. Often the same sentence reworded.
0.80 to 0.95Strongly related. This is your retrieval target zone.
0.60 to 0.80Same topic area. Useful, sometimes noise.
below 0.60Probably unrelated for most corpora.
Gotcha

These bands are corpus and model specific. Never hardcode a similarity threshold from a blog post. Measure it on your own data with your own questions.

In practice

What the call actually looks like

embed.py
from openai import OpenAI
client = OpenAI()

resp = client.embeddings.create(
    model="text-embedding-3-small",     # 1536 dims, cheap
    input=["annual leave policy", "how do I file expenses"]  # batch!
)
vectors = [d.embedding for d in resp.data]
print(len(vectors), len(vectors[0]))   # 2 1536
Batch it

One request with 100 inputs beats 100 requests by an order of magnitude on both latency and cost.

Fixed width

A 3 word query and a 400 word chunk both return 1536 floats. That is what makes them comparable.

Model selection

Which embedding model, and does it matter

ModelDimsHostingUse when
text-embedding-3-small1536OpenAI APIDefault starting point. Cheap, fast, good enough for most internal corpora.
text-embedding-3-large3072OpenAI APIMeasurable quality gain on hard, technical or multilingual corpora. Costs more to store.
voyage-31024Voyage APIStrong on code and long technical documents.
bge-m3 / e5-large1024Self-hostData cannot leave your network, or volume makes per-token pricing hurt.
all-MiniLM-L6-v2384Self-host, tinyPrototyping on a laptop with no API key. Runs on CPU.
Gotcha

Changing the embedding model means reindexing every document. Dimensions and geometry both change. Treat the choice as a migration, and store the model name alongside every vector so you can tell what is stale.

Before you move on

Four ways people break this

01

Mismatched models

Indexed with one model, queried with another. Scores become meaningless noise, and nothing throws an error.

02

Forgetting to normalise

If your database uses dot product rather than cosine, unnormalised vectors let long documents dominate every result.

03

Ignoring query prefixes

Models like e5 and bge expect "query: " and "passage: " prefixes. Skipping them quietly costs accuracy.

04

Embedding raw junk

Navigation menus, cookie banners and page footers embed just fine and then pollute every result set. Clean before you embed.

Level 3 · 20 minutes
03

Chunking:
where projects quietly fail.

Most disappointing RAG systems are not held back by the model or the database. They are held back by documents cut in the wrong places.

The necessity

Why we cannot just embed the whole document

01 · LIMITS

Models have input caps

Embedding models accept a few thousand tokens. A 40 page handbook does not fit, full stop.

02 · BUDGET

Prompts have budgets

Even with a large context window, stuffing whole documents costs money and measurably degrades attention.

03 · PRECISION

One vector, one meaning

A whole document averages into a single point that represents every topic weakly and none of them well.

The mental image

Embedding a whole handbook is a semantic smoothie. Leave, expenses, laptops and VPN rules all blend into one bland vector that matches every query equally badly.

The core tension

Too small loses context. Too large loses precision.

TOO SMALL · ~100 tokens

Chunk 41: "...up to 5 days."

Retrieved perfectly. Utterly useless. Five days of what? The subject was in the previous chunk and the model cannot see it.

TOO LARGE · ~2000 tokens

Chunk 3: "Chapter 4: Benefits, Leave, Equipment, Travel..."

Contains the answer somewhere. Also contains four unrelated policies. Weak vector, and the model has to find the needle itself.

Practical starting point

500 to 800 tokens per chunk, with 10 to 15 percent overlap. Then measure and adjust. This is a starting point, not an answer.

The toolkit

Five strategies, in order of effort

StrategyHow it cutsVerdict
Fixed sizeEvery N characters, blindlyBaseline only. Splits mid sentence and mid word.
RecursiveTry paragraph, then sentence, then word, until it fitsThe right default. Respects natural boundaries.
Structure awareSplit on markdown headings, HTML tags, code blocksBest value when documents have real structure.
SemanticEmbed sentences, cut where meaning shiftsSlow and costs embeddings up front. Sometimes worth it.
Parent documentIndex small chunks, return their larger parentBest of both. Precise search, full context.
If you only remember one

Recursive splitting on structure, with parent document retrieval when context keeps getting cut off. That combination handles most internal corpora.

In practice

Recursive splitting, the sane default

chunk.py
from langchain_text_splitters import RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter

splitter = RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter(
    chunk_size=700,
    chunk_overlap=100,
    separators=["\n## ", "\n\n", "\n", ". ", " "],  # strongest first
    length_function=count_tokens,   # NOT len()
)
chunks = splitter.split_text(doc.text)
Gotcha

The default length_function is len, which counts characters. Your model counts tokens. On code or non-English text the two diverge by a factor of two or more, and your chunks silently overflow.

The part beginners skip

A chunk is text plus everything you know about it

{
  "text": "Employees receive 24 days...",
  "source": "HR-Handbook-2025.pdf",
  "section": "4.1 Annual Leave",
  "page": 12,
  "updated_at": "2025-11-03",
  "visible_to": ["all-staff"],
  "lang": "en"
}
  • source and section let you cite, which is how users learn to trust the answer.
  • updated_at lets you prefer recent policy and expire old policy.
  • visible_to lets you filter per user at query time.
  • lang stops a German paragraph from answering an English question.
Say this out loud

A RAG system without permission metadata is a data leak that answers questions politely.

Level 4 · 20 minutes
04

Vector databases,
demystified.

A key value store with one unusual index type. That is honestly the whole idea. The interesting part is how it searches without looking at everything.

Anatomy

One record, four fields

{
  "id": "hr-handbook-2025#c41",        # stable, dedupable
  "values": [0.031, -0.118, ...],     # the 1536 floats
  "metadata": {
      "text": "Employees receive 24 days...",
      "source": "HR-Handbook-2025.pdf",
      "section": "4.1",
      "visible_to": ["all-staff"]
  }
}
  • id derived from a content hash makes re-indexing idempotent. Same content, same id, harmless overwrite.
  • values is the only field that needs a special index.
  • metadata.text matters: the database is what you read the prompt context from.
Gotcha

Storing only vectors and keeping text elsewhere means every search needs a second round trip, and the two stores drift out of sync. Keep the text next to the vector.

The interesting part

Exact search is correct and far too slow

BRUTE FORCE

10M chunks × 1536 dims
≈ 15 billion operations per query

Perfectly accurate. Hundreds of milliseconds to seconds. Unusable at scale.

APPROXIMATE · HNSW

≈ a few thousand comparisons
single digit milliseconds

Finds the true nearest neighbours around 95 to 99 percent of the time. That is a trade worth making.

Enter at the sparse top layer, take long hops toward the target, then drop down and refine locally. A greedy walk, not a scan.

Configuration

Four settings, and everything else is a default

KnobTypicalWhat it controls
metriccosineHow similarity is computed. Must match how you normalised your vectors.
dimensions1536Fixed by your embedding model. Changing it means a full reindex.
top_k20 → rerank to 4Candidates fetched. Retrieve generously, then narrow with a reranker.
ef_search64 to 256How hard HNSW looks. Higher means better recall and slower queries.
The one number to internalise

Retrieve more candidates than you intend to use. Fetching 20 and reranking to 4 beats fetching 4 directly, almost every time, for a few milliseconds of cost.

Tool choice

Five options, mostly the same API

PINECONE

Fully managed

No servers to run, scales without thought, serverless pricing. You are renting, and your data lives there.

WEAVIATE

Hybrid native

Keyword and vector search in one query, built in. Self-host or cloud. Strong schema and module system.

PGVECTOR

Just Postgres

An extension on the database you already run. Join vectors against your real tables. Usually the pragmatic answer.

CHROMA

Local first

pip install and go. Perfect for prototypes and this workshop. Not what you run in production at scale.

QDRANT

Rust, filter heavy

Excellent filtered search performance and quantization. Self-host friendly.

ALSO

Milvus, LanceDB, Elastic

All viable. Elastic and OpenSearch matter if you already run them for logs.

Retrieval:
from demo to trustworthy.

Plain similarity search gets you a demo. Hybrid search, reranking and careful prompt assembly get you something people rely on.

The funnel

Five stages, narrowing at every step

01Transformrewrite the query
02Retrievedense + sparse
03Fusemerge rankings
04Rerankcross-encoder
05Assemblebuild the prompt
CORPUS

10,000+ chunks

Everything you indexed.

CANDIDATES

20 to 50

Cheap search, generous recall. Precision does not matter yet.

CONTEXT

3 to 5 passages

Expensive scoring, high precision. This is what the model reads.

Cheap and broad first, expensive and narrow last. Never invert this order.

Two kinds of search

Each one fails where the other succeeds

DENSE · EMBEDDINGS

Matches meaning

  • Finds annual leave when you ask about vacation
  • Handles paraphrase, synonyms and other languages
  • Fails on exact rare tokens: ERR_4021, SKU-88X, surnames
SPARSE · BM25

Matches words

  • Nails exact identifiers, codes, names and quoted phrases
  • Cheap, explainable, needs no model and no GPU
  • Fails completely on paraphrase. No shared word means no match
Therefore

Run both, then merge the two ranked lists. This is the single highest value upgrade to a naive RAG system, and it takes about an hour to add.

Merging rankings

Reciprocal Rank Fusion: ignore the scores, keep the ranks

Why not just add the scores

BM25 returns unbounded values like 14.7. Cosine returns 0.83. Add them and BM25 silently decides every result.

def rrf(rankings, k=60):
    scores = defaultdict(float)
    for ranked_list in rankings:
        for rank, doc_id in enumerate(ranked_list):
            scores[doc_id] += 1 / (k + rank + 1)
    return sorted(scores, key=scores.get, reverse=True)
DocDense rankBM25 rankRRF
A1—0.0164
B230.0320
C—10.0164
Read the table

B wins without topping either list, because it is the only document both methods agree on. Agreement is a strong signal, and RRF is how you cash it in.

The accuracy upgrade

Rerank: read the pair, do not just compare points

BI-ENCODER · RETRIEVAL

Embed separately

vec(query) · vec(doc)

Document vectors are precomputed, so search is milliseconds over millions of chunks. Query and document are never compared directly.

CROSS-ENCODER · RERANK

Read together

score(query, doc)

The model sees both texts at once and judges relevance directly. Much more accurate, far too slow for a whole corpus.

candidates = hybrid_search(query, k=25)          # fast, generous
ranked     = cohere.rerank(query=query, documents=candidates,
                            model="rerank-v3.5", top_n=4)  # slow, precise

Typically the largest single quality jump after hybrid search, for roughly 100 to 200ms of added latency.

Fixing the question

The user's question is often a poor search query

TechniqueBeforeAfter
Follow-up rewrite"what about contractors?""what is the annual leave entitlement for contractors"
Multi-query"laptop policy"3 variants searched in parallel, results fused with RRF
HyDE"vpn rules"generate a fake ideal answer, embed that, search with it
Decomposition"compare leave and sick policy"two sub-questions, retrieved separately, answered together
Gotcha

In a chat product the follow-up rewrite is not optional. Embedding "what about contractors?" on its own retrieves noise, because the actual subject only exists in the previous turn.

The last mile

The prompt is where retrieval becomes an answer

prompt.py
SYSTEM = """Answer using ONLY the numbered context below.
Cite every claim as [1], [2]. If the context does not contain
the answer, say exactly: "I could not find this in our docs.""""

context = "\n\n".join(
    f"[{i}] ({c.source} §{c.section})\n{c.text}"
    for i, c in enumerate(chunks, 1)
)
messages = [{"role":"system", "content": SYSTEM},
            {"role":"user", "content": f"{context}\n\nQuestion: {question}"}]

An explicit escape hatch is what stops confident invention.

Numbered sources

Makes citations easy to emit and easy to verify.

Question last

The vector store is the only shared component, which makes it the right thing to build first.

Step 1 of 3

Ingest: documents in, vectors out

ingest.py
splitter = SentenceSplitter(chunk_size=700, chunk_overlap=100)
store    = ChromaVectorStore(chroma_collection=collection)

nodes = []
for path in Path("docs").rglob("*.md"):
    text = clean(path.read_text())          # strip nav, footers, boilerplate
    doc  = Document(text=text, doc_id=sha256(text),  # idempotent re-runs
        extra_info={"source": path.name, "dept": path.parent.name,
                    "updated_at": mtime(path), "visible_to": ["all-staff"]})
    nodes += splitter.get_nodes_from_documents([doc])

index = VectorStoreIndex(nodes, storage_context=ctx(store))  # embeds in batches
Why the content hash

Unchanged documents produce the same doc_id, so re-running ingest is a harmless overwrite. You can put this on a cron and stop thinking about it.

Step 2 of 3

Query: retrieve generously, rerank hard

query.py
retriever = index.as_retriever(
    similarity_top_k=25,                       # generous recall
    filters=MetadataFilters(filters=[
        MetadataFilter(key="visible_to", value=user.role, operator="in")
    ]),
)
engine = RetrieverQueryEngine.from_args(
    retriever,
    node_postprocessors=[CohereRerank(top_n=4)],   # precision pass
    response_mode="compact",
)
resp = engine.query("how many vacation days do I get?")
print(resp.response, [n.metadata["source"] for n in resp.source_nodes])

Twenty-five in, four out. That is the funnel from level 5, expressed as two arguments.

Chat: make follow-ups work

The part nobody demos

Measure retrieval and generation separately

RETRIEVAL · did we find it
  • Hit rate: is the correct chunk anywhere in the top k
  • MRR: how high up did it land
  • Context precision: what fraction of retrieved text was actually relevant
GENERATION · did we use it
  • Faithfulness: is every claim supported by the context
  • Answer relevance: did it address the question asked
  • Refusal accuracy: does it decline when the answer is genuinely absent
Start here, today

Hand-write 20 real questions with known correct sources. Measure hit rate first. That single number tells you whether to invest in chunking, retrieval or prompting next, and it takes an afternoon.

Triage

One question splits every failure in two

Always ask first

Was the correct chunk present in the retrieved context? Log the retrieved ids on every request, and this takes ten seconds to answer.

NO · A RETRIEVAL BUG
  • Is the document even indexed? Check ingest logs first
  • Chunk too small or split mid-fact? Revisit level 3
  • Paraphrase gap? Add hybrid search
  • Filter too aggressive? Check the empty-result path
YES · A GENERATION BUG
  • Too many passages? Cut to 3 or 4 and retest
  • Answer buried mid-context? Reorder, best passage last
  • Contradictory chunks? Add recency preference
  • Prompt too permissive? Tighten the refusal instruction
Before real users

Eight things that will bite you

01

Deletion

Removed documents must leave the index, or you quote dead policy forever.

02

Permissions at query time

Filter by the caller's identity, never by a hardcoded role.

03

Cost

Cache embeddings by content hash. Batch everything. Watch reranker spend.

04

Latency budget

Embed, search, rerank, generate. Know your p95 for each stage.

05

Observability

Log query, rewritten query, retrieved ids, scores and the answer. All of them.

06

Feedback

A thumbs down next to the citation is your cheapest evaluation dataset.

07

Empty results

Say you do not know. Never hand the model an empty context and hope.

08

Reindex plan

You will change models or chunk size. Make it a scripted, repeatable job.

Level 6 complete

Five sentences, if you forget everything else

  • RAG hands the model something to read instead of asking it to remember.
  • Embeddings turn meaning into coordinates, which is why paraphrase matching works at all.
  • Chunking decides your ceiling. Cut on structure, overlap a little, keep the metadata.
  • Hybrid search plus a reranker is the best quality per hour of work you can spend.
  • If you are not measuring hit rate, you are guessing about everything else.
